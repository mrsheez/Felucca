# Merge upstream, then own the clock

As of 30 October 2026 the FM-1 landscape has moved past EDDA OS 1.1.5 on three fronts. Felucca has shipped **seven tagged releases** since the 1.1.5.1 that EDDA forks: 1.4 through 1.7.0, adding step conditions, per-track inserts, MIDI learn, a beta second core and a new hardware layer. Roughly a hundred community firmwares now compete on dual cores, eight tracks and live scenes. M-VAVE has answered with stock V16 (eight parts, twenty voices) and an official open branch, FM1 Open. EDDA's claimed parity with SLOOP 2.4 is therefore three weeks out of date. The merge of 1.7.0 now under way is the right move, but it collides with EDDA in five places: the panel map, a settings byte, seven MENU ids, a palette index and the flash sector at 0xE7000. It also runs short of space. Upstream's own build already fills **513,044 of the 581,564-byte app slot**, so THE ARRIVAL's ~66 KB and the visualisers' ~17 KB cannot both stay compiled in. The hardware is a dual-core JieLi AC791N run at 360 MHz, with 512 KiB of SRAM, 1 MiB of flash, a working Bluetooth/Wi-Fi radio, full-speed USB, and **no analog input and no TRS MIDI out**. It supports a second core (with a fault on some units), BLE MIDI and USB audio in both directions, but not Ableton Link inside a synth firmware or sampling from a line input. The Omnis-Duo has no MIDI port, no LAN and no clock output, so a true tempo-and-phase lock exists only in laptop mode: rekordbox's Ableton Link, bridged to MIDI clock on the FM-1's USB. Without a laptop the FM-1 must be beatmatched like a third deck, and EDDA cannot do that yet. Its tempo moves in whole BPM and runs **0.04 % fast**, because it counts 44,100 samples a second on hardware that plays 44,117.6. None of the firmwares or flagship grooveboxes reviewed ships deck-grade tempo and phase tools, mix-in and mix-out macros timed to the phrase, or a log-drum engine. All three fit this chip's budget, and they, not track count, are where EDDA can lead. Before the new unit's first custom install, back up its whole flash: EDDA's fourth sample slot overlaps the factory radio calibration and Bluetooth address, and EDDA OS has never yet run on an FM-1.

## Felucca shipped seven releases in nineteen days

Felucca's version numbers hide its pace. 1.2 and 1.3 were internal stages that shipped together as **1.4 on 9 October**, which the wiki calls "the first release since 1.1.5.1". 1.5.5 and 1.5.6 were folded into 1.6 and never tagged ([Upgrading from 1.1.5](https://github.com/hugelton/Felucca/wiki/Upgrading-from-1.1.5); [1.6 commit](https://github.com/hugelton/Felucca/commit/1727537)). The tags are **v1.4, v1.4.1 and v1.5 (all 9 October), v1.5.1 (10 October), v1.6 (13 October), v1.6.1 (15 October) and v1.7.0 (28 October)**, and main is now three commits past the last of them ([v1.7.0 tag](https://github.com/hugelton/Felucca/commit/d7b1b7c); [65b21d3](https://github.com/hugelton/Felucca/commit/65b21d3)). EDDA OS 1.1.5 branched at ac6bfcb on 8 October, the commit right after 1.1.5.1, so it has none of the 32 upstream commits made since ([ac6bfcb](https://github.com/hugelton/Felucca/commit/ac6bfcb)). The feature work sits in 1.4–1.6; 1.6.1 and 1.7.0 are mostly architecture.

**The second core is real but cautious.** Since 1.6.1 every build includes it, and MENU > SYSTEM > 2ND CORE turns it on from the next power-on. Core 1 then renders a cost-balanced subset of the four parts into its own integer sums while core 0 renders the rest, so the output is bit for bit the one-core sound. Only part rendering is split: the master effects, the FX layer and the limiter stay on core 0 ([fx.c](https://github.com/hugelton/Felucca/blob/65b21d3/firmware/src/fx.c)). The feature remains **beta and off by default in 1.7.0**, because "on some FM-1s core 1 reads words that are not in RAM". Felucca retires core 1 on the first bad check word, fault or late job and switches the setting off ([fm1_cpu1.h](https://github.com/hugelton/Felucca/blob/a53ecf4/firmware/hal/fm1_cpu1.h); [987b205](https://github.com/hugelton/Felucca/commit/987b205)). No commit publishes how much headroom the second core buys.

The ADPCM engine committed after the tag shows why headroom matters. It is an SNES-style sample voice whose DSP is ported from snes_spc and tested bit for bit against it. Each voice costs about three ANALOG voices: eight voices take **54 % of a core against ANALOG's 17 %**, and four ADPCM parts miss the 2.9 ms deadline, so it stays out of default builds ([5d9694d](https://github.com/hugelton/Felucca/commit/5d9694d); [eng_adpcm.c](https://github.com/hugelton/Felucca/blob/65b21d3/firmware/src/eng_adpcm.c)). Those figures were taken on an FM-1 running 1.7.0. They are the only on-device figures found after the hardware-layer move, whose own final step reads "Not yet run on a device" ([5e84685](https://github.com/hugelton/Felucca/commit/5e84685)).

**The formats moved too, and that decides interoperability.** Projects went from FUN9 to "FUNA" (FUN10), a whole 3,840-byte storage object. It holds 104 and later 111 parameters, 128 three-byte motion records, each step's nudge in the top bits of its four note bytes, and an 84-byte chain of per-track song rows. Conditions and per-track MIDI channels later took spare bytes inside the same format ([project.c](https://github.com/hugelton/Felucca/blob/65b21d3/firmware/src/project.c)). Firmware older than 1.2 refuses FUN10 and shows such a slot as EMPTY, so **EDDA OS 1.1.5 cannot open any project saved by Felucca 1.4 or later**. The editor protocol gained seven new INFO blocks, and its MENU grew from 18 items to 33 ([EDITOR_PROTOCOL.md](https://github.com/hugelton/Felucca/blob/65b21d3/web/EDITOR_PROTOCOL.md)). The package identity did not change (FM-1_900, loader ota-FM-1_900), so installers still treat the two as one family. 1.6.1 added a size check that refuses another board's package before anything is erased, and 1.7.0's loader accepts M-VAVE's own V15 writer again ([a53ecf4](https://github.com/hugelton/Felucca/commit/a53ecf4); [5e84685](https://github.com/hugelton/Felucca/commit/5e84685)).

**Upstream is slowing down, which makes now the cheapest moment to merge.** On 16 October the author posted a notice stepping back from active development. On 30 October he deleted it and recast Felucca as "evolving toward a multi-platform architecture, separating its synthesis engines from hardware-specific implementations… without committing to specific ports or release schedules", adding a platform table that invites ports ([3a91058](https://github.com/hugelton/Felucca/commit/3a91058); [6fda396](https://github.com/hugelton/Felucca/commit/6fda396); [65b21d3](https://github.com/hugelton/Felucca/commit/65b21d3)). Since 1.6.1 almost every change has been either a community pull request or code moving into one of two new libraries. Felucca-Engines is under MIT. felucca-bsp is under MPL-2.0 and is now a multi-author base, run day to day by Charles Vestal, that several firmwares build on ([Release Notes](https://github.com/hugelton/Felucca/wiki/Release-Notes); [felucca-bsp README](https://github.com/hugelton/felucca-bsp/blob/main/README.md)). fm1-base states that a GPL-3.0 Felucca-based firmware can move onto fm1-base and felucca-bsp and keep its licence ([fm1-base README](https://github.com/charlesvestal/fm1-base/blob/main/README.md)), so EDDA can use both as libraries instead of porting their code.

### Five collisions and a full app slot shape the 1.7 merge

The merge collides with EDDA OS 1.1.5 in five places, each checked in both source trees.

The first is **the panel map**. EDDA's GLO + D4 starts the RUN and GLO + E4 is the hard stop, while upstream binds GLO + D4 to MIDI LEARN and GLO + E4 to FILL. Upstream's HOME button now carries the HOME, MIXER and CLOCK pages, where a tap on HOME in EDDA opens the visualisers ([EDDA-OS.md §3](/home/claude/Felucca/EDDA-OS.md); [129a4cf](https://github.com/hugelton/Felucca/commit/129a4cf); [9f6a885](https://github.com/hugelton/Felucca/commit/9f6a885); [88f1030](https://github.com/hugelton/Felucca/commit/88f1030)).

The second is **the settings byte**. EDDA keeps its MENU > EDDA preferences in byte [15][26] of the favourites block. Upstream gave that byte to the HOME view in 1.3 and has since used most of the neighbouring bytes, [15][25] included ([EDDA settings_persist.c](/home/claude/Felucca/firmware/src/settings_persist.c); [upstream settings_persist.c](https://github.com/hugelton/Felucca/blob/65b21d3/firmware/src/settings_persist.c)).

The third is **the editor protocol**. EDDA's seven MENU items sit at ids 18–24 in a tab at index 3. Upstream now uses those ids for SCOPE, STEP PREVIEW, CHORD ENTRY, MIDI IN, TUNE, HOME and HELP, puts its MIDI tab at index 3, and runs on to id 32 ([EDDA EDITOR_PROTOCOL.md](/home/claude/Felucca/web/EDITOR_PROTOCOL.md); [upstream EDITOR_PROTOCOL.md](https://github.com/hugelton/Felucca/blob/65b21d3/web/EDITOR_PROTOCOL.md)).

The fourth is **the palette**. COLOR value 10 is the EDDA palette in EDDA and the CUSTOM palette upstream (same two files).

The fifth is **flash**. EDDA's fourth sample slot starts at 0xE7000, the sector upstream 1.4.1 gave to the FM6 voice bank ([EDDA fm1_flash.h](/home/claude/Felucca/firmware/hal/fm1_flash.h); [fm6_vbank.c](https://github.com/hugelton/Felucca/blob/65b21d3/firmware/src/fm6_vbank.c)).

Each collision has a cheap answer. Keep EDDA's live gestures where the hands already know them: RUN on D4, STOP on E4 and FILL on A4 in the GLO layer. Move MIDI LEARN, a setup action, into the MIDI tab beside LEARN CLEAR, and give VIZ a gesture of its own. Move the EDDA preferences to a byte upstream leaves free, checked against upstream's whole map. Add EDDA's MENU items after id 32, in a tab of their own. Give the EDDA palette index 11, or ship it as the default CUSTOM palette.

For flash, move USR4 up one sector to 0xE8000–0xFBFFF. It still fits inside the BSP's XDATA1 window (0xE7000–0xFC000), because 80 KiB from 0xE8000 ends exactly at 0xFC000 ([felucca-bsp fm1_flash.h](https://github.com/hugelton/felucca-bsp/blob/main/chip/fm1_flash.h)). Upstream's storage layer allows writes to only one sector outside the store ([75290d8](https://github.com/hugelton/Felucca/commit/75290d8)), so that window has to be widened for USR4. Either placement still overlaps stock V15's radio calibration at 0xE8000 and the classic Bluetooth address at 0xE9000 ([BLE-HW-FACTS.md](https://github.com/charlesvestal/fm1-base/blob/main/docs/bt/BLE-HW-FACTS.md)). Community radio stacks recalibrate at every boot, but nobody knows whether stock V15 rebuilds lost calibration. That is why the whole flash should be backed up before USR4 is ever written.

Beyond the collisions, several EDDA features now duplicate upstream work, and the rest have no upstream equivalent — they are why the fork exists.

| EDDA OS 1.1.5 feature | What upstream 1.7 has | What the 1.7-based EDDA should do |
|---|---|---|
| Micro timing, −3…+3 sixteenths of a step | NUDGE, −8/16…+7/16 of a step, stored in FUN10 | Adopt upstream's; EDDA's range maps into it without loss |
| Fill-only steps, FILL on GLO + A4 | FILL and !FILL conditions alongside 1:2…4:4, FILL on GLO + E4 | Adopt the conditions and keep one FILL key |
| MUTATE (seeded Euclidean BELL lane on the next one) | EUCLID tool (HITS, STEPS, ROT) | Keep MUTATE as the live gesture, built on upstream's Euclid |
| Dotted echoes 1/4D 1/8D 1/16D | The same delay times (1.5.1) | Drop EDDA's copy |
| SELECT PAGES (MENU id 24) | SELECT PAGE (MENU id 25) | Drop EDDA's copy |
| Exact grid for the internal clock | A tick-exact grid for the external clock (#269) | Keep EDDA's grid, adopt #269, then fix the time base (see the Omnis-Duo section) |
| Track DJ filter, SEQ OUT with clock, Camelot KEY, RUN, hard stop, REVEAL, show cues, CHORD+ and VOIC LEAD, 16-pad user kits, EDDA voice bank, VIZ, THE ARRIVAL | Not in the 1.4–1.7 release notes | Keep, with the keys, ids and storage moved as above |

Saved work needs the same care. EDDA 1.1.5 projects are FUN9 with EDDA's nudges and fill flags, which plain Felucca drops ([EDDA-OS.md §1.4](/home/claude/Felucca/EDDA-OS.md)). The 1.7-based EDDA should map them into FUN10's nudge bits and FILL condition when it loads them. THE ARRIVAL's arrangements were written for FUN9's single-slot chain. They fit naturally into upstream's per-track song lanes, where a resting track is a "-" in its row, so the songs' subtraction transitions become arrangement data ([88f1030](https://github.com/hugelton/Felucca/commit/88f1030)). EDDA's editor also has to move to the merged protocol, because upstream's hosted editor would mislabel EDDA's rows.

**Space is a harder limit than the collisions.** The last size upstream recorded before 1.7.0 is **App 513,044 B, RAM 61,828 B, pool 326,208 B** ([5e84685](https://github.com/hugelton/Felucca/commit/5e84685)). That leaves about **68.5 KB** of the 581,564-byte app slot, 11.9 KB of the 72 KiB RAM region and 42 KB of the 360 KiB pool. EDDA documents THE ARRIVAL's songs, kit and log drum at about 66 KB (the songs themselves 12 KB) and the visualisers at about 17 KB ([EDDA-OS.md §2.21, §6, §7](/home/claude/Felucca/EDDA-OS.md)). Those two alone exceed upstream's headroom before the RUN, the pattern bank or SEQ OUT are counted.

The sound data is the part to move. The EDDA kit and the LOG set are samples, and EDDA already plays 16-pad kits from user slots. Shipping them as editor uploads frees about 54 KB of the app slot, at the cost of a user slot or two. RAM is the second squeeze. EDDA 1.1.5's .data and .bss fill 93,688 bytes of the old 98,304-byte region, and 1.5 shrank that region to 72 KiB to grow the pool ([129a4cf](https://github.com/hugelton/Felucca/commit/129a4cf)). Whatever static buffers EDDA added must fit in upstream's remaining 11.9 KB or move into the pool.

## Rivals converged on dual cores, eight tracks and live scenes

The scene grew from a handful of firmwares at the start of October to roughly a hundred listed projects. One community directory counts about 67 in the Felucca ecosystem and 29 more built on SLOOP, and FW//RADAR lists 50 ([aroum directory](https://github.com/aroum/fm1-custom-fw); [FW//RADAR](https://fwradar.com/p/m-vave-fm-1)). One ranking blog counted "27 releases across four repos in about 3.5 days, then 24 more from 6 to 9 Oct" ([dreyandersson.com](https://dreyandersson.com/blog/m-vave-fm-1-custom-firmware/)). Much of this is AI-assisted, and there is a backlash. A Hacker News commenter calls most FM-1 firmware "mostly vibe-coded", and a Synthtopia reader called the SMK-37 ports "unchecked for trojans" ([HN summary](https://zeli.app/story/49994065); [Synthtopia, 12 Oct](https://www.synthtopia.com/?p=155241)).

**X0X** by Charles Vestal went from a beta on 4 October to 1.0, which uses both cores and cut its heaviest pattern from about 115 % to 68 % load, and reached 1.1.0 by 22 October. It is a TR-909, a TR-808, two TB-303s with TB-3PO generators and a breakbeat slicer, each with its own length, rate and swing. It has 64 steps, 32 patterns, p-locks, probability cycles, a drum mixer and a master compressor with a kick-keyed pump ([Synth Anatomy, 5 Oct](https://synthanatomy.com/2026/10/charles-vestal-x0x-custom-firmware-turns-the-m-vave-fm-1-into-a-rebirth-like-groovebox.html); [newreleases 1.0.4](https://newreleases.io/project/github/charlesvestal/fm1-x0x/release/v1.0.4)). From 1.0.4 it keeps running on one core when the second misreads, and shows "1 CORE: BAD READS". Its betas added 32-step undo, safe mode after two failed starts, and crash reports ([X0X releases](https://github.com/charlesvestal/fm1-x0x/releases)).

Vestal's other projects are infrastructure for the scene. FoMni (formerly OMNI) is an Omnichord-style instrument with chords on the black keys, strumming across the white keys and the OM-84's ten rhythms ([Synthtopia, 12 Oct](https://www.synthtopia.com/?p=155230)). Polly is a QY70-style arranger, and fm1-base is a base other firmwares build on ([aroum directory](https://github.com/aroum/fm1-custom-fw)). X0X's start and mailbox code sits inside Felucca's second core ([a53ecf4](https://github.com/hugelton/Felucca/commit/a53ecf4)).

**The two closed firmwares sell safety and design.**

**Groove OS** ($29, Peter Gombos) is an eight-track groovebox with a twenty-voice pool (12 FM, 8 VA) and tracks that loop at their own lengths. It has drum flam, eleven trig conditions, per-track MIDI out, and clock in and out. Its perform page drops tracks in and out from the white keys, launches scenes from the black keys and fires eight punch-in effects from the top keys ([Synth Anatomy, 4 Oct](https://synthanatomy.com/2026/10/groove-os-turns-the-m-vave-fm-1-into-an-8-track-groovebox.html)). A video shows version 1.3 by 12 October. One blog reports 1.4 and says it patches stock V15 rather than replacing it. Reader comments dwell on the price and on "vibe-coded" quality ([MATRIXSYNTH, 12 Oct](https://www.matrixsynth.com/2026/10/m-vave-fm-1-groove-os-13-update-drum.html); [dreyandersson.com](https://dreyandersson.com/blog/m-vave-fm-1-custom-firmware/); [Synthtopia, 4 Oct](https://www.synthtopia.com/?p=155069)).

**FM-1+VA** (Baud Girl, free, closed) keeps M-VAVE's boot code and FM engine, fixes how the engine plays DX7 patches, and adds a supersaw VA voice and a 303-style 64-step sequencer. Its installer requires stock V15 and refuses any file whose first 16 KB differ from M-VAVE's. The open-source release promised in September had still not appeared by build 102 ([Sonicstate](https://sonicstate.com/news/2026/09/29/free-custom-firmware-for-mvave-fm-1-/); [Synth Anatomy](https://synthanatomy.com/2026/09/baud-girl-fm-1-va-custom-m-vave-fm-1-firmware.html); [dreyandersson.com](https://dreyandersson.com/blog/m-vave-fm-1-custom-firmware/)).

**The late-October newcomers show where the frontier is heading.**

**Melodee 1.5.1** replaces Felucca's engines with a Prophet-5 emulation (eight voices a track on two cores, five on one), Dexed FM, CZ-1, SID and drums. It runs up to eight tracks, the last four driving other synths through USB host, and returns to USB update mode after an interrupted write ([Melodee wiki](https://github.com/keremimo/melodee/wiki); [Melodee README](https://github.com/keremimo/melodee)). **fm1-dock** (26 October) loads four synth engines that swap on the fly, lets users deploy their own module over USB in seconds, and adds BLE MIDI ([Synthtopia, 26 Oct](https://www.synthtopia.com/content/2026/10/26/latest-fm-1-firmware-lets-you-load-4-synth-engines-swap-them-on-the-fly/)).

Obervave turns the FM-1 into an eight-voice Oberheim-style polysynth ([Synth Anatomy, 15 Oct](https://synthanatomy.com/2026/10/zvenson-obervave-your-m-vave-fm-1-and-smk-37-pro-elite-are-now-oberheim-like-polysynths.html)). Mutable Dreams 1.1 makes it a six-track generative instrument built on Plaits, Rings and Elements ([MATRIXSYNTH, 26 Oct](https://www.matrixsynth.com/2026/10/mutable-dreams-11-warmer-voices-your.html)). Synthesthetic streams its visuals to a computer as a USB webcam, FM1 Move adds clips and scenes, NoteSorcery exports to Ableton, and Felucca MPE adds MPE ([aroum directory](https://github.com/aroum/fm1-custom-fw)). Genkigenki's port kit carries FM-1 firmware to M-VAVE's SMK-37 keyboard ([Synthtopia, 12 Oct](https://www.synthtopia.com/?p=155241)).

Recovery has hardened alongside. Safe modes, an OCT− rescue at power-on, protection against interrupted writes and one-core fallbacks are now standard in the leading firmwares ([newreleases SLOOP 2.8](https://newreleases.io/project/github/isod89/sloop-fm1/release/v2.8); [Melodee wiki](https://github.com/keremimo/melodee/wiki); [X0X releases](https://github.com/charlesvestal/fm1-x0x/releases)).

**The territory EDDA occupies is still empty.** The directory lists genre firmwares for psytrance (MYCELIUM, SPIRALFM1), jungle (BREAK//174), dub and reggae (REGGAE-OS, DUB-1), and maqam and dastgah (sardab, Simorgh). None is for afro house, amapiano or West African idioms, and none has a key lock for harmonic mixing, DJ transitions, an album's songs with live cues, or cues for lights ([aroum directory](https://github.com/aroum/fm1-custom-fw)). Groove OS's perform page and SLOOP's LIVE page are the closest performance layers, and both are built for jamming rather than for a set played over records.

### M-VAVE's V16 and FM1 Open reset the stock baseline

M-VAVE stopped being a bystander in the second half of October.

**Stock V16 (21 October)** makes the factory synth eight-part multitimbral, each part on its own MIDI channel. It raises polyphony from 12 to **20 shared voices** and extends patterns from 16 to 64 steps in an arrangement of eight tracks by eight columns. Its six effects become stereo in one global chain. It also adds per-part fine tune, pitch-bend range and glide, DX7-standard .syx bank transfer, and RGB colour schemes ([MATRIXSYNTH, 23 Oct](https://www.matrixsynth.com/2026/10/m-vave-fm-1-official-v16-firmware.html); [FW//RADAR](https://fwradar.com/p/m-vave-fm-1)).

Separately, **FM1 Open**, announced around 17–20 October, is an official open branch: repository m-vave/fm1_open, release tag V14, numbered independently of the stock firmware. Its application source is GPLv3 with an SDK linking exception that covers M-VAVE's binary engine. It runs an eight-track, 64-step sequencer over DX7 (128 voices in four groups of 32), 808 KIT, 303 BASS, PLUCK, PSG and a sampler whose eight slots share about 19.55 seconds. Its browser Workbench previews, flashes, force-recovers and restores stock V15 ([MATRIXSYNTH, 20 Oct](https://www.matrixsynth.com/2026/10/m-vave-fm1-open-v14-official-open.html); [Synth Anatomy, 17 Oct](https://synthanatomy.com/2026/10/m-vave-fm-1-open-the-green-light-for-custom-firmware-and-community-hacks.html); [NeoTeo](https://www.neoteo.com/en/m-vave-fm-1-firmware-why-stock-v16-and-open-v14-are-separate)). M-VAVE says it "is providing its own framework for extending the instrument's capabilities" ([Synthtopia, 20 Oct](https://www.synthtopia.com/?p=155393)).

Two consequences follow for EDDA. First, V16 gives every buyer eight parts and twenty voices without custom firmware, so track count no longer sets anyone apart. Second, Felucca and EDDA are GPL-3.0-only without M-VAVE's exception, so linking M-VAVE's binary SDK into EDDA would need permission from Felucca's copyright holders. That second point is an inference from the two licences, not legal advice.

**The scene is still pinned to stock V15.** SLOOP, X0X, Melodee, Felucca and EDDA all document V15 as the way back to stock ([SLOOP wiki](https://github.com/isod89/sloop-fm1/wiki); [X0X releases](https://github.com/charlesvestal/fm1-x0x/releases); [Melodee wiki](https://github.com/keremimo/melodee/wiki); [EDDA-OS.md §1.4](/home/claude/Felucca/EDDA-OS.md)). FM-1+VA's installer requires V15, and Groove OS reportedly patches it ([Sonicstate](https://sonicstate.com/news/2026/09/29/free-custom-firmware-for-mvave-fm-1-/); [dreyandersson.com](https://dreyandersson.com/blog/m-vave-fm-1-custom-firmware/)).

No source documents a change to the USB update protocol in V16; the one V16 breakage found is a Linux updater that expected V15 to be M-VAVE's current download ([fuleo issue #1](https://github.com/fuleo/fm1-linux-update/issues/1)). But no custom firmware documents a test on a V16 unit either, and units in the field run different stock versions; Ars Technica's test unit was on V14 ([NeoTeo](https://www.neoteo.com/en/m-vave-fm-1-firmware-why-stock-v16-and-open-v14-are-separate)). For a unit bought in October, the documented path is to find out which stock firmware it carries. If it is V16, return it to V15 with M-VAVE's M-UPGRADE before installing anything Felucca-based ([FW//RADAR](https://fwradar.com/p/m-vave-fm-1)), and keep a copy of the V15 .fwsc file, because M-VAVE's download page now serves V16. Felucca 1.6.1 refused M-VAVE's V15 package with error −15 until 1.7.0 relaxed its loader ([Discussion #274](https://github.com/hugelton/Felucca/discussions/274)), one more reason for the 1.7-based EDDA to carry the 1.7 loader.

The safety net is solid. The mask-ROM USB mode ("WL82 UBOOT1.00") survives any firmware write. The MvaveFM1Unbricker for Windows and Linux takes and verifies a **full-flash backup** before it writes anything, and never touches the 16 KB boot head ([Unbricker wiki](https://github.com/Quixotic7/MvaveFM1Unbricker/wiki)). FM-1-transporter forces ROM boot through an RP2040 when the update disk never appears ([FM-1-transporter](https://github.com/kurogedelic/FM-1-transporter)). A backup taken on the first day is also how this unit keeps its factory radio calibration and Bluetooth address.

## The AC791N offers two cores and a radio, but no audio input

Every source that names the chip calls it JieLi's **AC791N**, internal name **WL82**. It is a dual-core 32-bit pi32v2 part with a single-precision FPU, 32 KB of instruction cache and 24 KB of data cache, and an integrated 2.4 GHz 802.11b/g/n radio with Bluetooth 5.0 ([AC7911B datasheet](https://atta.szlcsc.com/upload/public/pdf/source/20241209/7B2C0C261932781E1CA5530337098F4C.pdf); [fm1_open fm1_audio.h](https://github.com/m-vave/fm1_open/blob/main/app/board/fm1_audio.h)). The datasheet rates it to 320 MHz, but M-VAVE's bootloader runs it at **360 MHz**, and felucca-bsp restores that clock when another bootloader has left the chip at 320 MHz ([fm1_clock.h](https://github.com/hugelton/felucca-bsp/blob/main/chip/fm1_clock.h)).

The FM-1 has **512 KiB of SRAM and no PSRAM or SDRAM**; M-VAVE's own linker sets SDRAM_SIZE = 0. Code executes in place from a **1 MiB Puya P25Q80 SPI flash** in a replaceable SOIC-8 package ([fm1_open linker.ld.in](https://github.com/m-vave/fm1_open/blob/main/sdk/config/linker.ld.in); [felucca-bsp fm1_flash.h](https://github.com/hugelton/felucca-bsp/blob/main/chip/fm1_flash.h)). The loader writes applications only below 0x93000, which fixes the app slot at 581,564 bytes. Projects, samples and presets share a 292 KiB store, and an 84 KiB XDATA1 window sits above the autosave ([FM-1-transporter](https://github.com/kurogedelic/FM-1-transporter); [Felucca app.ld](https://github.com/hugelton/Felucca/blob/main/firmware/app.ld)). At 360 MHz and the real 44,117.6 Hz frame rate, each core gets **8,160 cycles per stereo frame**, about 1.04 million per 2.9 ms block of 128 frames. That figure is computed from the clock and the 544 timer ticks per frame in [audio.c](https://github.com/hugelton/Felucca/blob/main/firmware/src/audio.c).

**Polyphony today is a design ceiling more than a silicon one.** Felucca shares one budget of **eight voices** across its four parts, and fades a voice after two blocks in a row above 85 % load ([core.h](https://github.com/hugelton/Felucca/blob/main/firmware/src/core.h); [audio.c](https://github.com/hugelton/Felucca/blob/main/firmware/src/audio.c)). On an FM-1, eight ANALOG voices on one part cost 17 % of a core ([eng_adpcm.c](https://github.com/hugelton/Felucca/blob/65b21d3/firmware/src/eng_adpcm.c)). That puts a light voice at about 170 cycles per sample at most, so one core has cycles for on the order of forty such voices. RAM per voice, the reverbs and cache misses on code run from flash bind long before that.

The second core roughly doubles capacity for work that splits by part. Melodee's Prophet-5 goes from five voices a track to eight ([Melodee README](https://github.com/keremimo/melodee)), and X0X's heaviest pattern drops from 115 % to 68 % ([newreleases 1.0.4](https://newreleases.io/project/github/charlesvestal/fm1-x0x/release/v1.0.4)). But core 1 misreads shared RAM under load on some units ([felucca-bsp fm1_cpu1.h](https://github.com/hugelton/felucca-bsp/blob/main/chip/fm1_cpu1.h)).

Two more marginal faults appear at the bootloader's settings. Inlined 32×32→64-bit multiplies placed among 16-bit loads now and then leave a register one bit off ([CPU.md](https://github.com/hugelton/felucca-bsp/blob/main/docs/CPU.md)). And some units misread flash and SRAM under load at the bootloader's 1.26 V core supply, which V15 raises to 1.38 V ([fm1_sys.h](https://github.com/hugelton/felucca-bsp/blob/main/chip/fm1_sys.h)). SLOOP 2.7 sets V15's supply levels at boot for exactly this reason ([v2.7](https://github.com/isod89/sloop-fm1/releases/tag/v2.7)), and Melodee removed its overclock menu after some units were left stuck in the ROM update mode ([Melodee README](https://github.com/keremimo/melodee)).

EDDA's own static count from JieLi's disassembly already puts its audio interrupt 19 % above Felucca's, and two of its clock functions 29–59 % above. Nobody has checked those figures on a device ([EDDA-OS.md §7](/home/claude/Felucca/EDDA-OS.md)).

**The I/O is narrower than the chip.**

Audio leaves through an external codec on the ALNK0 I2S port, at 24 bits and 44,117.6 Hz, to the 3.5 mm stereo jack and the speaker. M-VAVE's own board code states that the FM-1 **has no connected analogue input** ([fm1_open fm1_audio.h](https://github.com/m-vave/fm1_open/blob/main/app/board/fm1_audio.h); [felucca-bsp fm1_audio.h](https://github.com/hugelton/felucca-bsp/blob/main/boards/fm1/fm1_audio.h)).

MIDI comes in over TRS (input only, a UART at 31,250 baud), USB and Bluetooth, and goes out only over USB and Bluetooth ([fm1_open README](https://github.com/m-vave/fm1_open/blob/main/README.md); [fm1_uart.h](https://github.com/hugelton/felucca-bsp/blob/main/chip/fm1_uart.h)).

USB is full-speed. felucca-bsp provides class-compliant MIDI, CDC serial, UAC1 audio from the FM-1 to a computer at 44.1 or 48 kHz, UAC1 audio from the computer through the FM-1, and a USB MIDI host that needs an external 5 V supply ([usb.c](https://github.com/hugelton/felucca-bsp/blob/main/chip/usb.c); [USB_HOST.md](https://github.com/hugelton/felucca-bsp/blob/main/docs/USB_HOST.md)).

The 240×240 ST7789 display hangs off a 12 MHz SPI link, which limits full-screen animation to about twelve frames a second ([EDDA ui_viz.c](/home/claude/Felucca/firmware/src/ui_viz.c)). The carbon pills under all 27 keys can be read through the ADC for **velocity and pressure**. This reverse-engineered mode ships in CTL-1, and felucca-bsp leaves it off by default ([fm1_input.h](https://github.com/hugelton/felucca-bsp/blob/main/boards/fm1/fm1_input.h); [fm1-ctl](https://github.com/charlesvestal/fm1-ctl)).

**The radio is fitted and works.** Stock firmware advertises as FM-1_BLE and plays Bluetooth audio. Community firmware runs BLE MIDI in both roles, Bluetooth audio out to headphones, and Wi-Fi internet radio over HTTPS ([BLE-HW-FACTS.md](https://github.com/charlesvestal/fm1-base/blob/main/docs/bt/BLE-HW-FACTS.md); [fm1-radio README](https://github.com/charlesvestal/fm1-radio/blob/main/README.md)). Cost decides what fits.

fm1-base's own MPL-2.0 Bluetooth stack adds about **41–46 KB of flash and 10 KB of RAM** for BLE MIDI, against 124 KB and 45 KB for JieLi's binary stack. It holds one MIDI link at a time, and in the central role it uses an 11.25 ms connection interval. Bluetooth audio reaches the ear 150–300 ms after a key ([fm1-base README](https://github.com/charlesvestal/fm1-base/blob/main/README.md); [BLE-STACK.md](https://github.com/charlesvestal/fm1-base/blob/main/docs/bt/BLE-STACK.md)).

Wi-Fi costs about 300 KB of flash, 100 KB of RAM and 276 KB of pool, which leaves an application about 170 KB of flash and a few KB of RAM. A firmware gets Bluetooth or Wi-Fi, not both, and Wi-Fi builds must leave out USB audio ([fm1-base README](https://github.com/charlesvestal/fm1-base/blob/main/README.md)). The antenna reads about −75 dBm where a phone shows full bars ([fm1-radio manual](https://github.com/charlesvestal/fm1-radio/blob/main/app/docs/MANUAL.md)).

Ableton's Link library is C++ on asio, and its ESP32 port needs dual-core lock-free atomics and recommends a dedicated core ([esp_abl_link](https://components.espressif.com/components/docwilco/esp_abl_link/versions/4.0.0~2-beta2/readme)). **Link inside a synth firmware does not fit the FM-1.** The realistic forms are a stripped-down C implementation of Link's beat-time protocol in a dedicated clock firmware, or a phone or laptop bridging Link to the FM-1; neither has been demonstrated. Felucca 1.7.0 contains no radio code, and felucca-bsp's open Bluetooth drivers landed after the BSP revision 1.7.0 is built on ([felucca-bsp 606c9b2](https://github.com/hugelton/felucca-bsp/commit/606c9b2); [5e84685](https://github.com/hugelton/Felucca/commit/5e84685)).

## The Omnis-Duo emits no clock, so phase lock happens elsewhere

The Omnis-Duo was announced on 25 January 2024, and AlphaTheta's product page lists firmware 1.23 (4 December 2025) as current. It has a USB-C port for a computer, a USB-C power port, a USB-A storage port, an SD slot, Wi-Fi, and Bluetooth 5.2 for audio. It has **no MIDI DIN or TRS port, no LAN and no clock output** listed anywhere ([AlphaTheta product page](https://alphatheta.com/en/product/all-in-one-dj-system/omnis-duo/indigo/); [VirtualDJ rear panel](https://virtualdj.com/manuals/hardware/alphatheta/omnisduo/layout/frontrear.html)). AlphaTheta documents it as a MIDI controller for third-party software and never mentions clock ([AlphaTheta Help Center](https://support.alphatheta.com/en-US/articles/26559657575705?product=26559096570521)), and the 1.21 release notes add streaming services, not sync ([firmware 1.21](https://alphatheta.com/de/information/update-omnis-duo-firmware-ver-1-21/)). PRO DJ LINK tools cannot see it either: it has no LAN port, and the PRO DJ LINK Bridge list names only CDJs and DJMs ([rekordbox.com, Sept 2026](https://rekordbox.com/en/2026/09/rekordbox-for-mac-windows-and-pro-dj-link-bridge-support-soundswitch/)). One caveat stands. The Omnis-Duo's MIDI message list could not be retrieved, so the absence of USB clock is inferred from what the documentation leaves out, not read from the list.

| Route | Chain | Who sets the tempo | Phase | Needs |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| rekordbox Link | Omnis-Duo (USB-C) → rekordbox Performance mode with LINK on → Link-to-MIDI-clock bridge (Ableton Live, or an iOS app on a cable) → FM-1 USB MIDI | The Link session, set on rekordbox's Link subscreen, by TAP or from a mapped control; the TEMPO slider does not drive it | Link aligns beats; MIDI clock carries no bar, so start on the one or re-phase on the FM-1 | Laptop |
| rkbx_link | rekordbox master deck (read from memory) → Link → bridge → FM-1 | The record, through the pitch faders | Resyncs after 0.05 beat of error (27 ms at 112 BPM); delay compensation in ms | Windows laptop, rekordbox 7.1.4–7.2.19 |
| FM-1 leads | EDDA SEQ OUT +CLOCK → Ableton Live External Sync → Link → rekordbox decks on LINK | The FM-1 | The decks follow Link | Laptop with Live |
| Standalone | FM-1 audio → MIC 2; no data link | Whoever the DJ matches by ear | Manual | Firmware with fine tempo and nudge |

**The laptop routes all need a bridge.** rekordbox supports Ableton Link: turning on [LINK] changes each deck's BEAT SYNC into LINK, and a deck on LINK follows the session tempo, which is set on the Link subscreen, by TAP or by typing. With a hardware controller attached, the TEMPO slider cannot move it ([rekordbox FAQ](https://rekordbox.com/en/support/faq/ableton-link/)). rekordbox has no MIDI clock output, and neither does Serato. Serato DJ Pro supports Link, but the Omnis-Duo unlocks only DJ Lite ([DJ TechTools 2018](https://djtechtools.com/2018/06/27/serato-dj-pro-four-ways-for-syncing-with-external-gear/); [Serato](https://serato.com/dj/hardware/alphatheta-omnis-duo)).

Ableton Live can serve as the bridge: it joins the Link session and sends MIDI clock with a per-device Sync Delay. The iOS app Link To MIDI is reported to be tighter on a cable than bridges running over Wi-Fi ([Ableton Live 12 manual](https://www.ableton.com/en/live-manual/12/synchronizing-with-link-tempo-follower-and-midi/); [DJ TechTools 2018](https://djtechtools.com/2018/06/27/serato-dj-pro-four-ways-for-syncing-with-external-gear/)). rkbx_link reverses who leads. It reads the master deck's beatgrid from rekordbox's memory and publishes it to Link, with a delay compensation in milliseconds and a 0.05-beat resync tolerance. It supports Windows rekordbox 7.1.4–7.2.19, and its free evaluation licence covers only 7.2.2 ([rkbx_link](https://github.com/grufkork/rkbx_link)). That suits the Windows laptop EDDA already installs from ([EDDA-OS.md §1](/home/claude/Felucca/EDDA-OS.md)).

A phone could replace the cable by bridging the Link session to BLE MIDI. The FM-1 would then have to smooth jitter on the order of a BLE connection interval (11.25 ms in fm1-base's central role, [BLE-STACK.md](https://github.com/charlesvestal/fm1-base/blob/main/docs/bt/BLE-STACK.md)), which nobody has measured. Three questions remain open: whether rekordbox's Link aligns bars or only beats, whether Link needs a paid rekordbox plan when the Omnis-Duo unlocks Performance mode, and whether Serato DJ Lite has Link at all.

**EDDA OS 1.1.5 cannot yet be matched to records, and both reasons are in the code.**

The first is that its tempo is a whole number. The parameter runs from 40 to 240, and the tempo shown while following a clock is rounded to a whole number too ([params.c](/home/claude/Felucca/firmware/src/params.c); [midi_clock.c](/home/claude/Felucca/firmware/src/midi_clock.c)). A record at 112.5 BPM therefore sits half a BPM from the nearest setting, a 0.45 % error that flams by 10 ms in about two seconds, roughly one bar.

The second is that the internal clock counts a beat as FS × 60 / BPM with **FS = 44,100**, while the hardware plays 44,117.6 frames a second ([gen_tables.py](/home/claude/Felucca/tools/gen_tables.py); [audio.c](/home/claude/Felucca/firmware/src/audio.c); [fx.c](/home/claude/Felucca/firmware/src/fx.c)). Every tempo therefore runs **0.04 % fast**: the display's 112 is really 112.045 BPM. Against a record at exactly 112 it drifts about 24 ms a minute, enough for a flam within half a minute and a full sixteenth note in under six minutes.

Upstream 1.7 has the same whole-BPM parameter and the same 44,100 base ([upstream params.c](https://github.com/hugelton/Felucca/blob/65b21d3/firmware/src/params.c); [upstream fx.c](https://github.com/hugelton/Felucca/blob/65b21d3/firmware/src/fx.c)), so the merge does not fix either problem. It does bring three community clock fixes EDDA lacks:

| Fix | What it does | Source |
|---|---|---|
| #269 | Holds the external clock's phase on a fixed grid of 24,000 units per quarter note | [ebdf867](https://github.com/hugelton/Felucca/commit/ebdf867) |
| #258 | Timestamps TRS clock pulses at 2 kHz, even during long renders under CC load | [092f748](https://github.com/hugelton/Felucca/commit/092f748) |
| #264 | Fixes TRS input handling | [4d86e3d](https://github.com/hugelton/Felucca/commit/4d86e3d) |

The tempo fix itself is small. The frame rate is exactly 24,000,000 / 544 = 750,000/17 per second, which EDDA's exact grid can carry as its fraction. At hundredths of a BPM the numerator outgrows 32 bits, and 64-bit multiplies are exactly where this CPU's erratum sits, so the multiply helper should stay out of line, as felucca-bsp's workaround does ([CPU.md](https://github.com/hugelton/felucca-bsp/blob/main/docs/CPU.md)). With the true rate and hundredths of a BPM, a remaining 0.01 BPM error takes about fifty bars at 112 BPM to flam by 10 ms, against half a minute today.

In standalone sets the decks' jog wheels are better nudging tools than the FM-1's encoders. That suggests a natural split: while the FM-1 carries the groove it leads and the DJ matches records to it, and when it joins a record already playing it follows, using fine tempo, a hold-to-bend and a timed drop-in.

**The Omnis-Duo's inputs settle the audio path.** It has two mic channels, MIC1 on ¼-inch TRS and MIC2 on an XLR/TRS combo, under one MIC ON switch, with HIGH and LOW EQ at 10 kHz and 100 Hz (±12 dB) on the touchscreen. The only line input is Bluetooth, which one guide calls unsuitable for serious live performance ([AlphaTheta product page](https://alphatheta.com/en/product/all-in-one-dj-system/omnis-duo/indigo/); [DJGear2K](https://djgear2k.com/8-hidden-features-tricks-on-the-alphatheta-omnis-duo/)). Talkover must stay off, or every FM-1 note ducks the decks. Each mic input is mono, so EDDA's stereo spread collapses. Felucca's output ceiling of −6 dBFS leaves some headroom into a mic preamp, but both ends should start at minimum gain ([audio.c](https://github.com/hugelton/Felucca/blob/main/firmware/src/audio.c)). Beat FX can be assigned to the mic channel ([VirtualDJ controls](https://www.virtualdj.com/manuals/hardware/alphatheta/omnisduo/layout/controls.html)). That should put the Omnis-Duo's beat-synced effects on the FM-1 with no MIDI at all, provided they follow the deck tempo, which is unverified.

The only way the FM-1 could hear the decks without a laptop is over Bluetooth. The Omnis-Duo can send its master to a Bluetooth output, and M-VAVE's SDK reads Bluetooth audio on the FM-1 ([fm1_audio_io.h](https://github.com/m-vave/fm1_open/blob/main/sdk/include/fm1_audio_io.h)). A beat follower on that stream would need JieLi's closed Bluetooth stack, which a GPL-only firmware cannot ship without a linking exception ([fm1-radio notes](https://github.com/charlesvestal/fm1-radio/blob/main/notes/03-device-results.md)). It would also need RAM the FM-1 lacks, and compensation for Bluetooth latency that nobody has measured in that direction. It is a research project, not a roadmap item.

**Gear that got sync wrong shows what the firmware needs.** MIDI clock is 24 pulses per quarter note plus Start, Stop, Continue and a Song Position Pointer counted in sixteenths, with no bar marker ([MIDI beat clock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MIDI_beat_clock)). Link adds a quantum for bar phase and recommends launching on it ([Ableton Link docs](https://ableton.github.io/link)). Roland's TR-8S needed firmware 1.03 to stop its clock dropping out when five faders moved at once, and to make its effects follow external tempo ([Ask.Audio](https://ask.audio/articles/roland-releases-tr8s-103-update-fixes-tempo-sync-issue-new-improvements)). An Elektron Octatrack owner reports that tempo nudge does nothing while the machine follows external clock ([Elektronauts](https://www.elektronauts.com/t/nudge-tempo-when-midi-synced-to-external-source/109815)). Roland's TR-1000 flagship was reviewed with jitter on external sync ([MusicTech](https://musictech.com/reviews/hardware-instruments/roland-tr-1000-review/)).

The 1.7-based EDDA should therefore lock its steps to received ticks with upstream's #269 grid, and smooth the measured tempo only for the display and tempo-based effects. It should choose its clock source explicitly, as the TR-8S does with AUTO, USB, TRS and INT ([Roland support](https://support.roland.com/hc/en-us/articles/360050280652-TR-8S-TR-6S-Can-I-make-it-synchronize-with-DAW-software-or-an-external-MIDI-device)), and keep running at the last tempo through a dropped clock. It should accept a clock-only stream with the transport started by hand, and re-phase on a "this is the one" press while following. Finally, it should store an offset in milliseconds, because a MIDI tick at 112 BPM is 22 ms, too coarse to trim by.

## Deck-grade timing and amapiano macros lie past the frontier

Conditions, probability, micro-timing, ratchets, parameter locks, scales and song mode now ship on $299 boxes such as Sonicware's Deconstruct Minimal, and on Cre8audio's Programm sequencer ([MusicRadar, Superbooth 2026](https://www.musicradar.com/news/live/superbooth-2026-all-the-latest-synth-news-from-berlin-live)). The 1.7 merge brings most of them to EDDA from upstream. Flagships set themselves apart in the performance layer:

Teenage Engineering's EP-40 Riddim makes the other move. It is a whole device built around one genre's performance idiom, with a dub siren, twelve reggae punch-in effects and a dub-filter fader ([Dubspot](https://blog.dubspot.com/teenage-engineering-ep-40-riddim-review)).

**Amapiano gives EDDA the equivalent brief.** Production guidance puts the style at **108–115 BPM**. Its sixteenth-note shakers run continuously, varying in velocity, gaps and timing, and the shaker pattern changes every second or fourth bar. The log drum descends from FM (the eight-voice DX10) and is tuned to roots, fifths and octaves with a short decay. Kicks are removed where a heavy log-drum note lands, and sidechain stays light. Sections run in 8-, 16- and 32-bar phrases, and outros drop elements one by one for DJ mixing ([Beats To Rap On](https://beatstorapon.com/blog/how-to-make-amapiano-beat/)). Each of these is a job for sequencer events or control-rate parameters rather than audio buffers, which is why it is cheap on this chip.

One tension needs a decision, not a feature. THE ARRIVAL's rule is "everything on the grid (no swing)" ([EDDA-OS.md §2.21](/home/claude/Felucca/EDDA-OS.md)), while the guidance swings the shakers. Per-track swing and humanize belong in club sets as options, with the album's rules left as they are.

The feasibility labels are my estimates against the measured budgets above (the freed app slot, about 11.9 KB of RAM and 42 KB of pool upstream, 8,160 cycles a frame), not measurements. Rows 1, 2, 5 and 6 are the frontier. No FM-1 firmware or flagship reviewed documents a deck-style tempo nudge, a tap-timed start for rigs with no clock, mix-out macros timed to the phrase, or a log-drum engine with kick-yield. Each builds on something EDDA already does: the exact grid, the hard stop's re-entry on the one, the RUN, and the LOG sample set.

**Some of what EDDA should add comes from the scene rather than from upstream.** Late-October firmwares treat a safety net as standard: safe mode after two failed starts (X0X), a rescue key at power-on (SLOOP 2.8), a return to USB update mode after an interrupted write (Melodee), and a one-core fallback ([X0X releases](https://github.com/charlesvestal/fm1-x0x/releases); [newreleases SLOOP 2.8](https://newreleases.io/project/github/isod89/sloop-fm1/release/v2.8); [Melodee wiki](https://github.com/keremimo/melodee/wiki)). An instrument that gets reflashed between gigs needs the same. X0X's 32-step undo suits live editing ([X0X releases](https://github.com/charlesvestal/fm1-x0x/releases)). SLOOP records whole sets to WAV over USB audio, which suits a streamed set, and felucca-bsp already carries USB audio in both directions ([v2.7](https://github.com/isod89/sloop-fm1/releases/tag/v2.7); [usb.c](https://github.com/hugelton/felucca-bsp/blob/main/chip/usb.c)).

Synthesthetic shows the FM-1 can present itself to a computer as a USB webcam ([aroum directory](https://github.com/aroum/fm1-custom-fw)). That would put EDDA's twelve visualisers straight onto the stream machine, though the video would share the full-speed USB link with the audio. SEQ OUT should also reuse upstream 1.6's per-track channel settings, which today govern only MIDI in.

**Some things are not worth chasing.** Ableton Link on the device fails on memory, as the hardware section shows, and the Omnis-Duo does not speak Link itself. Sampling from a line needs an input the FM-1 does not have. On-device AI belongs to other hardware. The first neural-audio groovebox, Thoughtful Things' Engram, is a $770 Kickstarter prototype aimed at May 2027, and MPC Live III's stem separation runs on eight cores and 8 GB of RAM ([Gearnews](https://www.gearnews.com/thoughtful-things-engram-synth/); [Attack](https://www.attackmagazine.com/news/mpc-live-iii-is-here-with-3d-sensing-pads-step-sequencer/)). MIDI 2.0 can wait, since no groovebox with it turned up ([Synth Anatomy MIDI 2.0 tag](https://synthanatomy.com/tag/midi-2-0)). More tracks would spend scarce RAM to match what stock V16 and Groove OS already offer, when four tracks are enough for playing over records.

## Conclusion

The scene's three-week arms race converged on one design: dual cores, eight tracks, scenes and punch-ins. Then M-VAVE's stock V16 gave every buyer eight parts and twenty voices for free, so breadth stopped mattering before EDDA could chase it. None of roughly a hundred firmwares, and no flagship reviewed, offers a groovebox that behaves like a third deck. The most valuable step toward one is a correction rather than a feature. Counting time on the FM-1's real 750,000/17 Hz clock, in hundredths of a BPM, turns a box that drifts off a record within half a minute into one a DJ can match by the number. Upstream has the same error, so the fix is worth offering back, and sending EDDA's corrections upstream is also what keeps future merges cheap.

The merge also changes what THE ARRIVAL is. The app slot cannot hold upstream 1.7, the album's samples and the visualisers at once. Moving the kit and the log drum into data flash does more than make room: it turns the album into a song pack the editor uploads. That pack can grow with the next record without a reflash, which is fm1-dock's module idea applied to music instead of engines. EDDA has passed 3,546 host checks but has never run on an FM-1, and this chip's known faults (second-core misreads, the multiply erratum, flash misreads at low voltage) show up only on hardware. So the new unit's first job is to be measured, with 2ND CORE off and CPU load and drift logged against a record, before it plays a room.
